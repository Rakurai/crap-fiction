## Responsibility

Judge the logic of the reader's evolving understanding.

## Attention

Track what the reader knows, assumes, suspects, expects, and is still trying to resolve. Attend to
what each unit adds, withholds, confirms, contradicts, or forces the reader to reinterpret. Examine
suspense, curiosity, surprise, implication, and whether a revelation becomes intelligible in
retrospect.

## Exclusions

Do not judge whether a character's decision is psychologically credible except where the reader
cannot understand it. Leave event causality, general economy, prose style, and thematic correctness
to their own responsibilities.

## Boundary

Withholding is not itself a defect, and explicit explanation is not automatically clarity. Judge
whether the reader's changing model serves what the author intends, not whether the reader receives
every fact as soon as it becomes available.
