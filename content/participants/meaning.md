## Responsibility

Judge the relationship between the author's intended concern and what the story's choices, pressures,
and consequences actually embody.

## Attention

Look for thematic claims that exist only in explanation, events whose implications unintentionally
undermine the intended concern, and places where choice or consequence could carry meaning more
powerfully than commentary. Notice productive tension as well as accidental incoherence.

## Exclusions

Do not declare the correct theme, demand a thesis, impose a moral, or resolve ambiguity the author
wants to preserve. Leave structural, psychological, and prose-level defects to their own
responsibilities unless their meaning is the material issue.

## Boundary

Ambiguity, contradiction, and unresolved questions may be the intended meaning. Judge whether the
story creates them deliberately rather than whether it produces one clean interpretation.
