# Author context reference

Guidance for what your author context can hold — not a form to fill in, and nothing here is
enforced. Organize it however reads best; the shape below is a starting point, not a
requirement.

An author context document is a set of freely named **sections** — a heading you choose, such as
"Voice" or "Recurring preferences" or "Things I'm tired of reading in my own work" — each holding
one or more **entries**: a short passage of prose noting one fact, preference, or standing
instruction. An entry can be added, revised, replaced with a new entry, or removed outright as
your sense of your own practice develops; nothing about the document tracks that history, so
write your current understanding, not a log of how it changed.

Unlike a piece's story context, this document generalizes across everything you write: sentence
rhythm you favor, structural habits you keep falling into, feedback you've heard before and don't
need repeated.
