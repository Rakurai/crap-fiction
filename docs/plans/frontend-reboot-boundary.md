# Boundary Audit — Step 1 Deliverable

**Noncanonical.** This report governs nothing. It is the step-1 deliverable of `docs/plans/frontend-reboot.md`, and the canon that governs is `docs/VISION.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, `docs/PRD.md`, `docs/UX_DESIGN.md`, `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/INTERFACES.md`, read in that order — where two of those conflict, the earlier governs. Where this report and any of them disagree, they are right.

It records **ownership only**: where a decision is made today, not how it should be represented once it moves. Representation is step 4's and is deliberately left open.

Merged from three independent audits, with every disputed row verified against the code. **Ownership below is frozen.**

The third audit ran after this report's table had been frozen and after the plan's own table was corrected, so it read the corrected rows rather than the ones that caused the first two audits to disagree. It reaches every conclusion below independently, including the three that had to be argued out of the first two: that the settled discussion is served correctly and is not split, that the Markdown round trip is a module both sides must call, and that projecting a served arrival order is not a boundary violation. Its must-move list is the same three items. It raises nothing against the interviewer's invitation, the waiting state, or a no-comment response being shown — the three flags rejected below on the precedence rule.

`Actual` reads: `server` where the room or the store decides; `client` where the browser decides; `shared` where a derivation sits in a module neither side owns; `split` where one concern is decided in two places under two rules; `duplicated` where both sides compute the same fact. Consuming a served value is correct and is not a gap.

## Must move server-side

Frozen. Ownership only; nothing here prescribes a representation.

- **The addressable set for a surface.** Assembled in `src/client/EditingSurface.tsx` from three served fields rather than consumed as one served list, while the room derives its own ceiling independently. `PieceDetail` in `src/shared/pieceViews.ts` is why: it serves the ingredients and not the set, and among addressed-only participants it declares the interviewer specially, so the client cannot consume the room's set even in principle.
- **A conversation's opening words.** One fact through `openingWords` in `src/shared/conversationEntries.ts`, computed twice: by `listConversations` in `src/server/pieces.ts` for the conversations listing, and again by `conversationName` in `src/client/conversationNaming.ts` over the projected entries for the open conversation. Served once, consumed by both. **Author decision, recorded.**
- **The rule that makes a note absent.** `said` in `src/shared/participantResponse.ts`. Three lines, and the smallest item here, but it is a domain decision in the contract layer.

**The settled discussion was on this list and has been removed.** Both source audits reported it as split. It is not: its only consumer is context compilation, and the transcript renders the record rather than the settled discussion by design. See the row above.

## Corrections to the plan's own table

The audit found the table it was to be frozen against wrong in three rows. All three are corrected in `frontend-reboot.md`; recorded here because the corrections are step-1 findings.

- **Autosave persistence** was one row assigned to the server. It is two concerns: the client owns scheduling, debounce, the single write in flight, retry on the next ordinary write and the failure state; the server owns the durable atomic write. The third audit read the corrected pair of rows, was told to expect a conflict with `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` there, and found none — the correction holds.
- **The Markdown round trip and the constrained schema** were assigned wholly to the client. Both sides call the module, and the server's call is load-bearing for Apply.
- **Optimistic echo of the author's own message** was assigned to the client. No such behavior exists — the room mints and persists the entry and the client renders it on the appended-entry event. The row is deleted rather than reworded: adopting an optimistic echo is a step-4 protocol question, and asserting it as an established boundary would license inventing client state standing in for a server-authoritative fact.

## Canon defects found incidentally

Not boundary findings, and not this step's to fix. They fold into the step-2 canonical edit. Each is a place `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` contradicts `docs/UX_DESIGN.md`, which precedes it — so in each case the code is right and the architecture document is the stale side.

- **A no-comment response is shown.** `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` holds that a no-comment outcome is recorded and not shown. `docs/UX_DESIGN.md` requires it as one line in the participant's name, and `CONTEXT.md` places it in the conversation while absent from the *settled discussion* — which is a different thing from absent from the transcript. `src/client/Conversation.tsx` renders it, correctly.
- **A participant waiting to be called.** `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` holds that a client may not read a stage the room has not reported. `docs/UX_DESIGN.md` requires four states on a participant's line and "waiting to be called" is the first. The prohibition is properly about queue *position*, which nothing renders, and the wording should say so.

## Settled by the author

All four questions this audit raised are answered. Nothing remains unsettled.

- **The word count of an applied change is dropped.** No count of a change reaches the author. `src/client/Conversation.tsx` computes a per-passage maximum of before and after word counts for the closed disclosure label; that computation goes, and the closed state states that the change landed with no quantity — "applied", and "rewritten whole" where the change is unbounded. The server's own before, after, added and removed counts in `src/server/room/appliedChange.ts` stay: they decide whether a change is an unbounded rewrite, which is a rule about what the change file may hold rather than a label. **This requires a `docs/UX_DESIGN.md` edit** — that document currently mandates that the closed disclosure be a count of what was altered, and that the count be of words and exact. Both statements go, in the step-2 canonical edit.
- **The live length of the document and the served length of a piece are unrelated counts.** The editor shows the length of the text as it currently stands, including what is unsaved; the pieces listing shows the length the server derived from what is on disk. Neither owes the other agreement, so they are two facts rather than one with two authorities. `countWords` in `src/shared/storyLength.ts` being called from both sides is correct and is not a finding.
- **A conversation's opening words are served.** One fact from the server, consumed by both the conversations listing and the head of the open conversation, which must agree. Moved to the frozen list above.
- **A participant's display identity is resolved through the surface roster.** `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` already decides this: an event names a participant by identity and never by display name, the client resolves names through the roster, and a conversation does not render until the roster has landed. `src/client/useRoster.ts` resolving identity from the call-sites report is the inconsistency. The call-sites report's display name serves the model-assignment surface alone.

## Carried to step 4

Not ownership questions, and not this step's to answer. Recorded so they are not rediscovered.

- **Serving opening words needs a delivery decision.** The head of the open conversation needs the value for a conversation whose first author message has just landed over the stream, and the served conversation summary is not refetched on an appended entry. Either a frame carries it or the summary list refreshes. This is why it was not already served.
- **The client projects response arrival order and never re-derives it.** The room decides and durably records the order in which responses land. Whatever shape step 4 settles on must keep the projection a consumer of that order.

## Not a gap

`src/shared/conversationEntryViews.ts`, `src/shared/applyViews.ts`, `src/shared/callSiteViews.ts` and `src/shared/pieceViews.ts` are zod schemas and inferred types with no derivation in them. The shared placements this report records are `src/shared/participantResponse.ts` (`said`), `src/shared/handle.ts`, `src/shared/conversationEntries.ts` (`openingWords`) and `src/shared/storyLength.ts`.

Rejected as findings, each on the precedence rule: the interviewer invitation in `src/client/Conversation.tsx` composing a sigil and dispatching it as the author's message is explicitly permitted by `docs/UX_DESIGN.md` as a shortcut through the keyboard — a control that composes a message the author could have typed and sends it on exactly the terms that message would have carried. The `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` sentence against synthesizing a sigil governs reply and concrete-change dispatches, which carry an explicit target instead of text.
